## 1. Ratio

### 1.1 Definition

A **ratio** is a comparison of two quantities of the same kind expressed as a fraction.

> Ratio of a to b = **a : b = a/b**
> where a = **antecedent** (first term) and b = **consequent** (second term)

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Both quantities in a ratio must be in the **same unit** before comparison.
> Ratio has **no unit** — it is a pure number.

### 1.2 Key Properties of Ratio

| Property | Statement | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Multiplication | Multiply both terms by same non-zero number → ratio unchanged | 2:3 = 4:6 = 6:9 |
| Division | Divide both terms by same non-zero number → ratio unchanged | 12:18 = 2:3 |
| Order matters | a:b ≠ b:a (unless a=b) | 3:5 ≠ 5:3 |
| Ratio of equals | If a = b → a:b = 1:1 | 7:7 = 1:1 |
| Zero | 0 cannot be a consequent | 5:0 is undefined |

### 1.3 Types of Ratios

| Type | Definition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Ratio of Equality** | Both terms are equal | 5:5 |
| **Ratio of Greater Inequality** | Antecedent > Consequent | 7:3 |
| **Ratio of Lesser Inequality** | Antecedent < Consequent | 3:7 |
| **Duplicate Ratio** | Square of a given ratio | a²:b² is duplicate of a:b |
| **Triplicate Ratio** | Cube of a given ratio | a³:b³ is triplicate of a:b |
| **Sub-duplicate Ratio** | Square root of a given ratio | √a:√b is sub-duplicate of a:b |
| **Sub-triplicate Ratio** | Cube root of a given ratio | ∛a:∛b is sub-triplicate of a:b |
| **Inverse Ratio** | Reciprocal of a given ratio | b:a is inverse of a:b |
| **Compound Ratio** | Product of two or more ratios | (a:b) × (c:d) = ac:bd |

#### Example — Compound Ratio
> Compound ratio of (2:3), (4:5), (6:7)
> = (2×4×6) : (3×5×7)
> = 48 : 105
> = **16 : 35** ✅

#### Example — Duplicate Ratio
> Duplicate ratio of 3:4
> = 3² : 4²
> = **9 : 16** ✅

### 1.4 Comparing Ratios

To compare a:b and c:d:
- Cross multiply: compare **a×d** vs **b×c**
- If ad > bc → a:b > c:d
- If ad < bc → a:b < c:d
- If ad = bc → a:b = c:d

**Example:** Compare 3:4 and 5:7
- 3×7=21 vs 4×5=20
- 21 > 20 → **3:4 > 5:7** ✅

### 1.5 Dividing a Quantity in a Given Ratio

> If a quantity Q is divided in ratio a:b:
> - First part = Q × a/(a+b)
> - Second part = Q × b/(a+b)

**Example:** Divide ₹1200 in ratio 3:5
- First part = 1200 × 3/8 = **₹450**
- Second part = 1200 × 5/8 = **₹750** ✅

> If Q is divided in ratio a:b:c:
> - First part = Q × a/(a+b+c)
> - Second part = Q × b/(a+b+c)
> - Third part = Q × c/(a+b+c)

### 1.6 Finding Missing Term in a Ratio

> If a:b = c:d, and one term is unknown, cross multiply:
> **a×d = b×c**

**Example:** 3:x = 12:20
- 3×20 = x×12
- x = 60/12 = **5** ✅

## 2. Proportion

### 2.1 Definition

When two ratios are **equal**, the four quantities are said to be in **proportion**.

> **a : b = c : d** → written as **a : b :: c : d**
> Read as: "a is to b as c is to d"

- a and d are called **Extremes**
- b and c are called **Means**
- d is the **Fourth Proportional**

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Product of Extremes = Product of Means**
> → a × d = b × c
> This is the **fundamental rule of proportion** — tested directly in MCQs.

### 2.2 Types of Proportion

#### Continued Proportion
> Three quantities a, b, c are in **continued proportion** if:
> **a : b = b : c**
> → b² = ac
> → b is called the **Mean Proportional** between a and c

**Example:** Find mean proportional between 4 and 16
- b² = 4 × 16 = 64
- b = **8** ✅

#### Third Proportional
> If a : b = b : c, then c is the **third proportional** to a and b
> → c = b²/a

**Example:** Third proportional to 4 and 8
- c = 8²/4 = 64/4 = **16** ✅

#### Fourth Proportional
> If a : b = c : d, then d is the **fourth proportional** to a, b, c
> → d = (b × c)/a

**Example:** Fourth proportional to 3, 6, 9
- d = (6 × 9)/3 = 54/3 = **18** ✅

#### Mean Proportional
> Mean proportional between a and b = **√(a×b)**

**Example:** Mean proportional between 9 and 25
- = √(9×25) = √225 = **15** ✅

### 2.3 Direct Proportion

> As one quantity increases, the other increases **proportionally**.
> **x/y = constant (k)** → x = ky

**Example:** If 5 pens cost ₹25, then 8 pens cost:
- 25/5 = 5 per pen
- 8 × 5 = **₹40** ✅

### 2.4 Inverse Proportion

> As one quantity increases, the other **decreases** proportionally.
> **x × y = constant (k)** → x = k/y

**Example:** 6 workers finish a job in 10 days. 4 workers finish in:
- 6 × 10 = 4 × d
- d = 60/4 = **15 days** ✅

## 3. Properties of Proportion

These are standard algebraic operations applied to a:b::c:d (i.e., a/b = c/d):

| Property | Name | Formula | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| a×d = b×c | Cross multiplication | — | Fundamental rule |
| a/c = b/d | Alternendo | Swap b and c | a:c :: b:d |
| b/a = d/c | Invertendo | Flip both fractions | b:a :: d:c |
| (a+b)/b = (c+d)/d | Componendo | Add denominator to numerator | — |
| (a-b)/b = (c-d)/d | Dividendo | Subtract denominator from numerator | — |
| (a+b)/(a-b) = (c+d)/(c-d) | Componendo & Dividendo | Combine both | Most tested in MCQs |
| a/b = c/d = (a+c)/(b+d) | Addendo | Add numerators and denominators | — |
| a/b = c/d = (a-c)/(b-d) | Subtrahendo | Subtract numerators and denominators | — |

#### Componendo & Dividendo — Worked Example
> If a/b = 5/3, find (a+b)/(a-b)
> By Componendo & Dividendo:
> (a+b)/(a-b) = (5+3)/(5-3) = 8/2 = **4** ✅

> [!TIP]
> **Componendo & Dividendo** is the single most MCQ-tested proportion property. Learn to apply it in one step.

## 4. Variation

### 4.1 Direct Variation (Direct Proportion)

> x ∝ y → x = ky (k is constant of variation)
> **x₁/y₁ = x₂/y₂**

### 4.2 Inverse Variation (Inverse Proportion)

> x ∝ 1/y → xy = k
> **x₁y₁ = x₂y₂**

### 4.3 Joint Variation

> z ∝ xy → z = kxy
> If x increases and y increases → z increases proportionally to both

**Example:** If z varies jointly as x and y, and z=12 when x=3 and y=2:
- k = z/(xy) = 12/(3×2) = 2
- When x=5, y=4: z = 2×5×4 = **40** ✅

### 4.4 Partial Variation

> z = kx + c (partly constant, partly varying with x)
> Need two conditions to find k and c

## 5. Partnership

### 5.1 Definition

A **partnership** is a business arrangement where two or more individuals invest capital and share profits/losses in proportion to their investments.

### 5.2 Types of Partners

| Type | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Active / Working Partner** | Invests capital AND manages the business. May receive salary/commission before profit division |
| **Sleeping / Dormant Partner** | Invests capital only — no involvement in management |
| **Minor Partner** | Can share profits but liability is limited |

### 5.3 Simple Partnership (Same Time Period)

> All partners invest for the **same duration**.
> Profit is shared in **ratio of investments** only.

> **Profit of A : Profit of B = Investment of A : Investment of B**

**Example:**
A invests ₹30,000, B invests ₹50,000 for the same period.
Total profit = ₹16,000. Find each partner's share.

- Ratio = 30,000 : 50,000 = **3:5**
- A's share = 16,000 × 3/8 = **₹6,000**
- B's share = 16,000 × 5/8 = **₹10,000** ✅

### 5.4 Compound Partnership (Different Time Periods)

> Partners invest for **different durations**.
> Profit is shared in ratio of **Investment × Time**.

> **Profit ratio = (Capital of A × Time of A) : (Capital of B × Time of B)**

**Example:**
A invests ₹20,000 for 6 months, B invests ₹30,000 for 4 months.
Total profit = ₹9,000. Find each share.

- A's equivalent = 20,000 × 6 = 1,20,000
- B's equivalent = 30,000 × 4 = 1,20,000
- Ratio = 1,20,000 : 1,20,000 = **1:1**
- Each gets = 9,000/2 = **₹4,500** ✅

### 5.5 Partnership with Working Partner Salary

> If one partner gets a salary/commission first, subtract it from total profit FIRST, then divide remaining profit in the investment ratio.

**Example:**
A and B invest ₹40,000 and ₹60,000. Total profit = ₹25,000.
A is the working partner and gets ₹2,000/month as salary.
Duration = 12 months. Find each share.

- Total salary = 2,000 × 12 = ₹24,000
- Remaining profit = 25,000 − 24,000 = ₹1,000
- Ratio = 40,000 : 60,000 = 2:3
- A's additional share = 1,000 × 2/5 = ₹400
- B's share = 1,000 × 3/5 = ₹600
- **A's total = 24,000 + 400 = ₹24,400** ✅
- **B's total = ₹600** ✅

### 5.6 Key Partnership Formulas

| Scenario | Formula |
|---|---|
| Same time, find ratio | P_A : P_B = C_A : C_B |
| Different time, find ratio | P_A : P_B = C_A×T_A : C_B×T_B |
| Find investment given profit | C ∝ P (when time is same) |
| Find time given profit | T ∝ P (when capital is same) |
| Working partner salary | Deduct from profit first, then distribute remainder |

### 5.7 Finding a Partner's Investment or Time

**Type 1 — Find investment when profit ratio and time are known:**
> C_A/C_B = (P_A/P_B) × (T_B/T_A)

**Example:**
A and B invest for 8 months and 6 months. Profit ratio = 4:3. Find investment ratio.
- C_A/C_B = (4/3) × (6/8) = 24/24 = **1:1** ✅

**Type 2 — Find time when profit ratio and investment are known:**
> T_A/T_B = (P_A/P_B) × (C_B/C_A)

**Example:**
A invests ₹10,000 and B invests ₹15,000. Profit ratio = 2:3. How long did each invest?
- T_A/T_B = (2/3) × (15,000/10,000) = (2/3) × (3/2) = **1:1**
- Both invested for the same time ✅

## 📌 Extra Notes

> [!NOTE]
> Everything below goes beyond the core classroom syllabus but is **directly MCQ-relevant** and cross-referenced from RS Aggarwal, M. Tyra, and CAT-level sources.

### 📌 Ratio — Advanced Concepts

#### Adding/Subtracting Same Number — Effect on Ratio

> If a:b is given, and we add x to both terms:
> - New ratio = (a+x):(b+x)
> - If a < b → adding x makes ratio closer to 1 (increases)
> - If a > b → adding x makes ratio closer to 1 (decreases)

**Example:** Ratio is 3:5. Add 2 to both. New ratio = 5:7.
- 3/5 = 0.6 and 5/7 ≈ 0.71 → ratio increased (moved closer to 1) ✅

> [!NOTE]
> Subtracting the same number from both terms has the opposite effect.

#### Ratio Remaining Constant — Multiplier Rule

> If a:b = c:d, then ka:kb = c:d for any non-zero k.
> Ratios are scale-free — multiplying both terms by same number preserves ratio.

#### Three Quantities in Ratio

> If A:B = m:n and B:C = p:q
> To find A:B:C → make B common:
> A:B:C = mp : np : nq

**Example:** A:B = 2:3 and B:C = 4:5. Find A:B:C.
- Multiply first ratio by 4: A:B = 8:12
- Multiply second ratio by 3: B:C = 12:15
- A:B:C = **8:12:15** ✅

#### Four Quantities — Combining Two Ratios

> If A:B = a:b, C:D = c:d
> Then (A×C):(B×D) = ac:bd (compound ratio)

### 📌 Proportion — MCQ-Trap Concepts

#### Proportional Division into Three Parts — Trap
> When dividing in ratio a:b:c, MCQ traps often ask for the **difference** between two parts or the **largest/smallest** part.

**Example:** ₹7,200 divided in 3:4:5. Find the largest share.
- Total parts = 12
- Largest share = 7,200 × 5/12 = **₹3,000** ✅

#### If Ratio Changes When a Number is Added/Removed

> Classic MCQ type:
> Present ratio of ages A:B = m:n
> After x years, ratio becomes p:q
> Set up: (A+x)/(B+x) = p/q → solve simultaneously

**Example:** A:B = 2:3. After 4 years, A:B = 3:4. Find present ages.
- Let A=2k, B=3k
- (2k+4)/(3k+4) = 3/4
- 8k+16 = 9k+12 → k=4
- A = **8 years**, B = **12 years** ✅

### 📌 Partnership — Advanced MCQ Types

#### When a New Partner Joins Mid-Way

> Original partners A and B run for full year.
> C joins at month m → C's time = (12−m) months.
> Apply C_A×T_A : C_B×T_B : C_C×T_C

**Example:**
A invests ₹50,000 for full year. B invests ₹40,000 from month 4.
Total profit = ₹19,800. Find B's share.
- A's equivalent = 50,000 × 12 = 6,00,000
- B's equivalent = 40,000 × 9 = 3,60,000
- Ratio = 6,00,000 : 3,60,000 = **5:3**
- B's share = 19,800 × 3/8 = **₹7,425** ✅

#### Profit Sharing When Partner Withdraws Capital

> If a partner withdraws part of capital at some point:
> Split their investment period into two parts.

**Example:**
A invests ₹60,000 for 12 months. B invests ₹80,000 for 6 months, then withdraws ₹20,000 for remaining 6 months.
- A's equivalent = 60,000 × 12 = 7,20,000
- B's equivalent = (80,000×6) + (60,000×6) = 4,80,000 + 3,60,000 = 8,40,000
- Ratio = 7,20,000 : 8,40,000 = **6:7** ✅

#### When Profit Ratio is Given — Find Investment Ratio

> If time is the same for all:
> Investment Ratio = Profit Ratio (direct)

> If time differs:
> Investment Ratio = (Profit Ratio) × (Inverse of Time Ratio)

### 📌 Ratio — Important Number Relationships

| Concept | Rule |
|---|---|
| If a:b = b:c | b is the geometric mean of a and c; b² = ac |
| If a/b = c/d | ad = bc (cross product rule) |
| If a:b = 2:3 and total = T | a = 2T/5, b = 3T/5 |
| To eliminate fractions in ratio | Multiply all terms by LCM of denominators |

#### Ratio with Fractions — MCQ Type
> Express 1/2 : 1/3 : 1/4 as a whole number ratio
> - LCM of 2, 3, 4 = 12
> - Multiply each by 12: 6 : 4 : 3
> - Ratio = **6:4:3** ✅

### 📌 Variation — MCQ-Relevant Rules

| If | Then |
|---|---|
| x ∝ y and y ∝ z | x ∝ z (transitive property) |
| x ∝ y and x ∝ z | x ∝ (y+z) |
| x = ky (direct) | k = x/y = constant; graph is a straight line through origin |
| xy = k (inverse) | k = xy = constant; graph is a hyperbola |

### 📌 Componendo & Dividendo — Deeper Application

> Given: (a+b)/(a-b) = x/y
> Then: a/b = (x+y)/(x-y)

**Example:** (x+y)/(x-y) = 5/3. Find x:y.
- By componendo & dividendo applied in reverse:
- x/y = (5+3)/(5-3) = 8/2 = **4:1** ✅

### 📌 Special Ratios to Know

| Ratio | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 1:1 | Equal sharing |
| 1:2 | Second is double the first |
| 22:7 | Approximation of π |
| 1:√2 | Side of square to its diagonal |
| 1:√3:2 | Sides of 30-60-90 triangle |
| 1:1:√2 | Sides of 45-45-90 triangle |

### 📌 Partnership — Common MCQ Traps

> [!WARNING]
> **Trap 1:** Profit ratio ≠ Investment ratio when time differs — always include time.
> **Trap 2:** If working partner gets a salary, subtract it BEFORE computing profit share. Forgetting this is the most common wrong answer in partnership MCQs.
> **Trap 3:** "Loss" is also shared in the same ratio as profit unless stated otherwise.
> **Trap 4:** If no time period is mentioned, assume all partners invested for the same duration.
> **Trap 5:** Total investment ratio ≠ individual investment unless total is given.

## ⚡ Quick Reference Cheatsheet

### Ratio Types at a Glance

```
Duplicate ratio of a:b     →  a²:b²
Triplicate ratio of a:b    →  a³:b³
Sub-duplicate ratio of a:b →  √a:√b
Sub-triplicate ratio of a:b→  ∛a:∛b
Inverse ratio of a:b       →  b:a
Compound ratio (a:b)×(c:d) →  ac:bd
```

### Proportion Quick Reference

```
Extremes × product  =  Means × product  →  ad = bc

Mean proportional between a and b  =  √(ab)
Third proportional to a and b      =  b²/a
Fourth proportional to a, b, c     =  bc/a

Componendo & Dividendo:
  Given a/b = c/d  →  (a+b)/(a-b) = (c+d)/(c-d)
```

### Combining Ratios (A:B and B:C → A:B:C)

```
Step 1: Make B term equal in both ratios
Step 2: Multiply first ratio by (B value in second ratio)
Step 3: Multiply second ratio by (B value in first ratio)
Step 4: Write A:B:C

Example: A:B = 2:3 and B:C = 4:5
→ A:B = 8:12 and B:C = 12:15
→ A:B:C = 8:12:15
```

### Partnership Formula Card

```
Same time:
  Profit ratio = Capital ratio

Different time:
  Profit ratio = Capital × Time

New partner joins at month m (out of 12):
  Their time = (12 - m) months

Working partner gets salary S per month:
  Deduct S × 12 from total profit first
  Remaining profit → split in capital ratio
```

### Variation Summary

```
Direct:   x/y = k    (graph: straight line through origin)
Inverse:  x × y = k  (graph: hyperbola)
Joint:    z = kxy    (z ∝ x and z ∝ y simultaneously)
```

### Fraction Ratio → Whole Number Ratio

```
Step 1: List all denominators
Step 2: Find LCM of denominators
Step 3: Multiply each fraction by LCM
Step 4: Result is the whole number ratio

Example: 1/2 : 1/3 : 1/4
LCM(2,3,4) = 12
→ 6 : 4 : 3
```

## 🔁 Session Revision Snapshot

### 5-Bullet TL;DR

- ✅ **Ratio** is a pure number comparison (a:b = a/b); both terms must be in same unit; ratio is unchanged when both terms are multiplied or divided by the same non-zero number
- ✅ **Proportion** (a:b::c:d) means product of extremes = product of means (ad=bc); mean proportional = √(ac); use cross-multiplication to find unknown terms
- ✅ **Componendo & Dividendo** — (a+b)/(a-b) = (c+d)/(c-d) — is the fastest and most MCQ-tested proportion property; learn to apply it in one step
- ✅ **Simple partnership** → profit shared in capital ratio; **Compound partnership** → profit shared in capital × time ratio; always multiply first, then find ratio
- ✅ **Working partner salary** → always deduct total salary from total profit FIRST, then divide remainder in capital ratio — forgetting this step is the #1 mistake in partnership MCQs

### MCQ-Likely Concepts — High Priority

| Concept | Why It's Exam-Likely |
|---|---|
| Product of extremes = product of means | Direct formula MCQ |
| Mean proportional between two numbers | √(ab) — commonly tested |
| Componendo & Dividendo | Most tested proportion property |
| Combining two ratios (A:B and B:C) | Make B common — classic MCQ |
| Compound partnership (capital × time) | Distinction from simple partnership is a trap |
| Working partner salary deduction | Most missed step in partnership MCQs |
| Fraction to whole number ratio conversion | LCM trick — clean MCQ format |
| New partner joining mid-year | Time calculation trap |
| Dividing in ratio a:b:c — finding specific part | Three-part division MCQ |
| Duplicate vs Compound ratio distinction | Definition MCQ — easily confused |
