## 📖 Full Explanations

---

### Q01 — Whole Number but NOT Natural Number
**Correct Answer: B) 0**

- **Why B is correct:** Natural numbers start from 1 → {1, 2, 3, ...}. Whole numbers include 0 → {0, 1, 2, 3, ...}. So 0 is the only number that is whole but not natural.
- **Why A is wrong:** 1 is both a whole number AND a natural number.
- **Why C is wrong:** −1 is an integer but neither a whole number nor a natural number.
- **Why D is wrong:** 2 is both a whole number AND a natural number.

---

### Q02 — Unit Digit of 8²⁵⁶
**Correct Answer: D) 6**

- **Why D is correct:** Cyclicity of 8 = 4. Cycle: {8, 4, 2, 6}. Divide 256 by 4 → remainder = 0. Remainder 0 → **last element** of cycle = **6**.
- **Why A is wrong:** 8 is unit digit of 8¹ (remainder 1 position).
- **Why B is wrong:** 4 is unit digit of 8² (remainder 2 position).
- **Why C is wrong:** 2 is unit digit of 8³ (remainder 3 position).

---

### Q03 — Unit Digit of 9⁵¹
**Correct Answer: B) 9**

- **Why B is correct:** Cyclicity of 9 = 2. Cycle: {9, 1}. Divide 51 by 2 → remainder = 1. 1st element of cycle = **9**.
- **Why A is wrong:** 1 is unit digit of 9^even powers (remainder 0 → last element = 1).
- **Why C is wrong:** 3 does not appear anywhere in the unit digit cycle of 9.
- **Why D is wrong:** 7 does not appear anywhere in the unit digit cycle of 9.

---

### Q04 — Digit with Same Unit Digit for ALL Powers
**Correct Answer: C) 5**

- **Why C is correct:** 5¹=5, 5²=25, 5³=125... Unit digit is always 5. Cyclicity = 1 → never changes. Same applies to 0, 1, and 6.
- **Why A is wrong:** 3 has cyclicity 4 — unit digit rotates through {3, 9, 7, 1}.
- **Why B is wrong:** 8 has cyclicity 4 — unit digit rotates through {8, 4, 2, 6}.
- **Why D is wrong:** 7 has cyclicity 4 — unit digit rotates through {7, 9, 3, 1}.

---

### Q05 — Unit Digit of (17²³ × 13¹⁵)
**Correct Answer: A) 1**

- **Why A is correct:** Unit digit of 17²³ → base unit digit = 7, cyclicity = 4, 23 mod 4 = 3 → 3rd in {7,9,3,1} = **3**. Unit digit of 13¹⁵ → base unit digit = 3, cyclicity = 4, 15 mod 4 = 3 → 3rd in {3,9,7,1} = **7**. Product unit digit = 3 × 7 = 21 → **1**.
- **Why B is wrong:** 3 is only the unit digit of 17²³ alone, not the final product.
- **Why C is wrong:** 7 is only the unit digit of 13¹⁵ alone, not the final product.
- **Why D is wrong:** 9 would result from 3 × 3 or 7 × 7 — neither combination is what we get here.

---

### Q06 — Group with Only Cyclicity-4 Digits
**Correct Answer: C) 2, 3, 7, 8**

- **Why C is correct:** All four digits 2, 3, 7, 8 have cyclicity 4. Their unit digit cycles have length 4.
- **Why A is wrong:** 0, 1, 5, 6 all have cyclicity 1 — their unit digits never change.
- **Why B is wrong:** 4 has cyclicity 2, and 9 has cyclicity 2 — neither has cyclicity 4.
- **Why D is wrong:** 2 and 8 have cyclicity 4 but 4 and 6 have cyclicity 2 and 1 respectively — mixed group.

---

### Q07 — Unit Digit of 6⁹⁹⁹
**Correct Answer: D) 6**

- **Why D is correct:** 6 has cyclicity 1. Unit digit of any positive power of 6 is always **6**. 6¹=6, 6²=36, 6³=216 — always ends in 6.
- **Why A is wrong:** 2 never appears as a unit digit of any power of 6.
- **Why B is wrong:** 8 never appears as a unit digit of any power of 6.
- **Why C is wrong:** 4 never appears as a unit digit of any power of 6.

---

### Q08 — Unit Digit of (1! + 2! + 3! + ... + 20!)
**Correct Answer: A) 3**

- **Why A is correct:** From 5! onwards, every factorial ends in 0 (due to the factor 5×2=10). So only the first four factorials contribute to the unit digit: 1!+2!+3!+4! = 1+2+6+24 = 33 → unit digit = **3**. Adding any 5!, 6!, ... 20! only adds 0s to the unit digit, keeping it **3**.
- **Why B is wrong:** 5 would mean 5! somehow contributes a unit digit — but 5! = 120, unit digit 0, no change.
- **Why C is wrong:** 7 has no basis — it would require the sum of first 4 factorials to end in 7, which it doesn't.
- **Why D is wrong:** 9 would require 1+2+6+24 = 33 to end in 9 — it ends in 3.

---

### Q09 — Correct Number Classification Statement
**Correct Answer: C) All natural numbers are integers**

- **Why C is correct:** Natural numbers {1,2,3,...} are a subset of integers {...,-2,-1,0,1,2,3,...}. Every natural number is also an integer. ✅
- **Why A is wrong:** 0 is a whole number but NOT a natural number. Natural numbers start from 1.
- **Why B is wrong:** Not all integers are whole numbers — negative integers (like −1, −2) are integers but not whole numbers.
- **Why D is wrong:** Not all rational numbers are integers — 1/2, 3/4 are rational but not integers.

---

### Q10 — Unit Digit of (2³ × 3⁴ × 4² × 5¹)
**Correct Answer: A) 0**

- **Why A is correct:** Any product containing both 2 and 5 as factors produces a 10 — making the unit digit **0**. Here 5¹ and 2³ are both present → their product has a factor of 10 → unit digit = **0**. No further calculation needed.
- **Why B is wrong:** 5 would be the answer only if there were no factor of 2 in the product — but 2³ and 4²=16 both contribute factors of 2.
- **Why C is wrong:** 2 appears only as a factor, not as the unit digit of the final product once 5 is involved.
- **Why D is wrong:** 4 similarly gets eliminated once 5 contributes a factor that pairs with 2 to produce 0.

> [!TIP]
> Shortcut: If a product contains **both a multiple of 2 and a multiple of 5**, the unit digit is always **0**. Check for 2-5 pairing first before doing cyclicity calculations.

---

### Q11 — Remainder of (17 × 23) ÷ 5
**Correct Answer: D) 1**

- **Why D is correct:** Remainder rule for products: Rem[(A×B)/N] = [Rem(A/N) × Rem(B/N)] mod N. Rem(17/5) = 2. Rem(23/5) = 3. Result = (2 × 3) mod 5 = 6 mod 5 = **1**.
- **Why A is wrong:** 3 = Rem(23/5) alone — the product rule was not applied.
- **Why B is wrong:** 2 = Rem(17/5) alone — the product rule was not applied.
- **Why C is wrong:** 4 = (2 × 3) − 2 — subtracting without basis; the mod operation gives 1, not 4.

---

### Q12 — Remainder of 5⁹⁹ ÷ 6
**Correct Answer: B) 5**

- **Why B is correct:** Rule: aⁿ ÷ (a+1) where n is odd → remainder = a. Here a = 5, a+1 = 6, n = 99 (odd) → remainder = **5**.
- **Why A is wrong:** 4 has no basis from the (a+1) remainder rule for this case.
- **Why C is wrong:** 1 applies when n is even (5⁹⁸ ÷ 6 → remainder = 1), not odd.
- **Why D is wrong:** 0 would mean 6 divides 5⁹⁹ exactly — impossible since 5 and 6 share no common factors.

---

### Q13 — Divisibility by 9 Implies Divisibility by...
**Correct Answer: A) Divisible by 3**

- **Why A is correct:** 9 = 3². Any number divisible by 9 must also be divisible by every factor of 9. Since 3 is a factor of 9, divisibility by 9 always guarantees divisibility by 3.
- **Why B is wrong:** Divisibility by 6 requires divisibility by both 2 and 3. A number divisible by 9 may be odd (e.g., 9, 27) — so divisibility by 6 is NOT guaranteed.
- **Why C is wrong:** 18 = 2 × 9. Again requires the number to be even — not guaranteed (e.g., 9 is not divisible by 18).
- **Why D is wrong:** 27 = 3³. A number divisible by 9 (like 18) is NOT necessarily divisible by 27.

---

### Q14 — Divisible by 4 but NOT by 8
**Correct Answer: C) 28**

- **Why C is correct:** 28 ÷ 4 = 7 ✅ (divisible by 4). 28 ÷ 8 = 3.5 ✅ (NOT divisible by 8). Last 3 digits of 28 = 028, and 028 ÷ 8 = 3.5 — confirms not divisible by 8.
- **Why A is wrong:** 48 ÷ 8 = 6 — divisible by both 4 and 8.
- **Why B is wrong:** 32 ÷ 8 = 4 — divisible by both 4 and 8.
- **Why D is wrong:** 64 ÷ 8 = 8 — divisible by both 4 and 8.

---

### Q15 — Last 2 Digits of 21⁴³
**Correct Answer: C) 61**

- **Why C is correct:** Base ends in 1. Unit digit = 1 always. Tens digit = (tens digit of base × unit digit of power) mod 10 = (2 × 3) mod 10 = 6 mod 10 = **6**. Last 2 digits = **61**.
- **Why A is wrong:** 01 would mean tens digit = 0, requiring 2 × 3 = 0 — impossible.
- **Why B is wrong:** 41 would mean tens digit = 4, requiring (2 × something) mod 10 = 4 → unit digit of power = 2 — but unit digit of 43 is 3, not 2.
- **Why D is wrong:** 81 would mean tens digit = 8, requiring (2 × 4) = 8 → unit digit of power would need to be 4 — but unit digit of 43 is 3.

---

### Q16 — Divisibility Rule for 25
**Correct Answer: D) Its last 2 digits are divisible by 25**

- **Why D is correct:** Since 100 = 4 × 25 is divisible by 25, only the last 2 digits of any number determine divisibility by 25.
- **Why A is wrong:** Checking only the last 1 digit works for 5, not 25. (e.g., 35 ends in 5 but is not divisible by 25.)
- **Why B is wrong:** Checking last 3 digits is the rule for 8, not 25.
- **Why C is wrong:** Digit sum rules apply to 3 and 9. No digit sum rule exists for 25.

---

### Q17 — Remainder of 2¹⁰ ÷ 7
**Correct Answer: C) 2**

- **Why C is correct:** By Fermat's Little Theorem, 2⁶ mod 7 = 1. So 2¹⁰ = 2⁶ × 2⁴ = 1 × 16 = 16. 16 mod 7 = 16 − 14 = **2**.
- **Why A is wrong:** 4 = 2², not the remainder of 2¹⁰ ÷ 7.
- **Why B is wrong:** 3 has no basis — 16 mod 7 = 2, not 3.
- **Why D is wrong:** 1 = 2⁶ mod 7 (Fermat's result) — but we have 2¹⁰, not 2⁶.

---

### Q18 — Divisibility of n(n+1)(n+2)
**Correct Answer: B) Always divisible by 6**

- **Why B is correct:** n(n+1)(n+2) is the product of 3 consecutive integers. Among any 3 consecutive integers, at least one is divisible by 2 and at least one is divisible by 3. So the product is always divisible by 2 × 3 = **6**.
- **Why A is wrong:** Not always divisible by 4. Example: 1×2×3 = 6, and 6 is not divisible by 4.
- **Why C is wrong:** Not always divisible by 8. Example: 1×2×3 = 6, not divisible by 8.
- **Why D is wrong:** Not always divisible by 12. Example: 1×2×3 = 6, not divisible by 12.

---

### Q19 — Bells Ringing Together
**Correct Answer: A) 9:40 AM**

- **Why A is correct:** LCM(6, 8, 10). 6=2×3, 8=2³, 10=2×5. LCM = 2³ × 3 × 5 = **40 minutes**. They next ring together 40 minutes after 9:00 AM = **9:40 AM**.
- **Why B is wrong:** 9:30 AM = 30 minutes later. LCM(6,8,10) ≠ 30. (30 is not divisible by 8.)
- **Why C is wrong:** 9:48 AM = 48 minutes later. LCM(6,8,10) ≠ 48. (48 is not divisible by 10.)
- **Why D is wrong:** 9:24 AM = 24 minutes later. LCM(6,8,10) ≠ 24. (24 is not divisible by 10.)

---

### Q20 — Largest Square Tile for 126 cm × 84 cm Floor
**Correct Answer: C) 42 cm**

- **Why C is correct:** The side length of the largest square tile = HCF(126, 84). 126 = 2 × 3² × 7. 84 = 2² × 3 × 7. HCF = 2 × 3 × 7 = **42 cm**.
- **Why A is wrong:** 21 = 3 × 7 — this is a common factor but NOT the **highest** common factor.
- **Why B is wrong:** 14 = 2 × 7 — again a common factor but not the highest.
- **Why D is wrong:** 63 = 9 × 7 — 63 does not divide 84 (84/63 = 1.33...), so it cannot tile the floor evenly.

### Q01 — Whole Number but NOT Natural Number
**Correct Answer: B) 0**

- **Why B is correct:** Natural numbers = {1, 2, 3, ...}. Whole numbers = {0, 1, 2, 3, ...}. The only number in whole but not in natural is **0**.
- **Why A is wrong:** 1 belongs to both natural and whole numbers.
- **Why C is wrong:** −1 is an integer — it is neither whole nor natural.
- **Why D is wrong:** 2 belongs to both natural and whole numbers.

---

### Q02 — Unit Digit of 8²⁵⁶
**Correct Answer: D) 6**

- **Why D is correct:** Cyclicity of 8 = 4. Cycle: {8, 4, 2, 6}. 256 ÷ 4 = 64, remainder = **0**. Remainder 0 → last element of cycle = **6**.
- **Why A is wrong:** 8 is unit digit at remainder position 1 (i.e., 8¹, 8⁵, 8⁹...).
- **Why B is wrong:** 4 is unit digit at remainder position 2 (i.e., 8², 8⁶...).
- **Why C is wrong:** 2 is unit digit at remainder position 3 (i.e., 8³, 8⁷...).

---

### Q03 — Unit Digit of 9⁵¹
**Correct Answer: B) 9**

- **Why B is correct:** Cyclicity of 9 = 2. Cycle: {9, 1}. 51 ÷ 2 = 25, remainder = **1**. 1st element of cycle = **9**.
- **Why A is wrong:** 1 is the unit digit of 9^even powers (remainder 0 → last element = 1). 51 is odd, not even.
- **Why C is wrong:** 3 never appears in the unit digit cycle of 9 at any position.
- **Why D is wrong:** 7 never appears in the unit digit cycle of 9 at any position.

---

### Q04 — Digit Unchanged Across ALL Powers
**Correct Answer: C) 5**

- **Why C is correct:** 5¹=5, 5²=25, 5³=125, 5⁴=625. Unit digit is always **5**. Cyclicity = 1. Same is true for 0, 1, and 6 — but among the options given, only 5 qualifies.
- **Why A is wrong:** 3 has cyclicity 4 — rotates through {3, 9, 7, 1}. It changes.
- **Why B is wrong:** 8 has cyclicity 4 — rotates through {8, 4, 2, 6}. It changes.
- **Why D is wrong:** 7 has cyclicity 4 — rotates through {7, 9, 3, 1}. It changes.

---

### Q05 — Unit Digit of (17²³ × 13¹⁵)
**Correct Answer: A) 1**

- **Why A is correct:**
  - Unit digit of 17²³ → base unit digit = 7, cyclicity = 4, 23 mod 4 = 3 → 3rd in cycle {7,9,3,1} = **3**
  - Unit digit of 13¹⁵ → base unit digit = 3, cyclicity = 4, 15 mod 4 = 3 → 3rd in cycle {3,9,7,1} = **7**
  - Product unit digit = 3 × 7 = 21 → unit digit = **1** ✅
- **Why B is wrong:** 3 is only the unit digit of 17²³ in isolation — the product changes it.
- **Why C is wrong:** 7 is only the unit digit of 13¹⁵ in isolation — the product changes it.
- **Why D is wrong:** 9 would require a product like 3×3 or 7×7 — neither is this case.

---

### Q06 — Group with Only Cyclicity-4 Digits
**Correct Answer: C) 2, 3, 7, 8**

- **Why C is correct:** Digits 2, 3, 7, 8 all have cyclicity 4. Their cycles are {2,4,8,6}, {3,9,7,1}, {7,9,3,1}, {8,4,2,6} respectively.
- **Why A is wrong:** 0, 1, 5, 6 all have cyclicity 1 — their unit digit never changes.
- **Why B is wrong:** 4 has cyclicity 2 ({4,6}), and 9 has cyclicity 2 ({9,1}) — not 4.
- **Why D is wrong:** 2 and 8 have cyclicity 4 correctly, but 4 has cyclicity 2 and 6 has cyclicity 1 — a mixed and incorrect group.

---

### Q07 — Unit Digit of 6⁹⁹⁹
**Correct Answer: D) 6**

- **Why D is correct:** 6 has cyclicity 1. Every positive integer power of 6 ends in 6. There is no exception.
- **Why A is wrong:** 2 never appears as a unit digit of any power of 6.
- **Why B is wrong:** 8 never appears as a unit digit of any power of 6.
- **Why C is wrong:** 4 never appears as a unit digit of any power of 6.

---

### Q08 — Unit Digit of (1! + 2! + ... + 20!)
**Correct Answer: A) 3**

- **Why A is correct:** From 5! onwards, every factorial contains the factor 5 × 2 = 10, making their unit digit 0. So only 1! through 4! affect the unit digit: 1 + 2 + 6 + 24 = 33 → unit digit = **3**. Adding 5! through 20! contributes zero to the unit digit. Final answer = **3**.
- **Why B is wrong:** 5 would require the sum of 1!+2!+3!+4! to end in 5 — it ends in 3 (33), not 5.
- **Why C is wrong:** 7 has no basis — 1+2+6+24 = 33, unit digit is 3 not 7.
- **Why D is wrong:** 9 would require the running sum to end in 9 at some point — 33 ends in 3.

---

### Q09 — Correct Number Classification Statement
**Correct Answer: C) All natural numbers are integers**

- **Why C is correct:** Natural numbers {1,2,3,...} are a proper subset of integers {...,−2,−1,0,1,2,...}. Every natural number is by definition also an integer.
- **Why A is wrong:** 0 is a whole number but NOT a natural number. Natural numbers begin at 1.
- **Why B is wrong:** Integers include negative numbers like −1, −2, −3... which are NOT whole numbers.
- **Why D is wrong:** Rational numbers include non-integer fractions like 1/2, 3/5 — these are not integers.

---

### Q10 — Unit Digit of (2³ × 3⁴ × 4² × 5¹)
**Correct Answer: A) 0**

- **Why A is correct:** The product contains both a factor of 2 (from 2³ and 4²) and a factor of 5 (from 5¹). Any product with at least one factor of 2 × 5 = 10 ends in **0**. No need to compute the full product.
- **Why B is wrong:** 5 would be the unit digit only if no factor of 2 paired with the 5 — but 2³ is present.
- **Why C is wrong:** 2 is already consumed in pairing with 5 to produce 10 — the final unit digit is 0.
- **Why D is wrong:** 4 similarly gets absorbed — once 10 is a factor, unit digit is 0.

> [!TIP]
> **Golden shortcut:** Before using cyclicity, always check if the product contains both a factor of **2 and 5**. If yes → unit digit = **0** instantly.

### Q11 — Remainder of (17 × 23) ÷ 5
**Correct Answer: D) 1**

- **Why D is correct:** Remainder rule for products: Rem[(A×B)/N] = [Rem(A/N) × Rem(B/N)] mod N. Rem(17÷5)=2, Rem(23÷5)=3. So (2×3) mod 5 = 6 mod 5 = **1**.
- **Why A is wrong:** 3 = Rem(23÷5) in isolation — the product remainder rule wasn't applied.
- **Why B is wrong:** 2 = Rem(17÷5) in isolation — same mistake.
- **Why C is wrong:** 4 would be (2×3)−2 — subtracting without any logical basis.

---

### Q12 — Remainder of 5⁹⁹ ÷ 6
**Correct Answer: B) 5**

- **Why B is correct:** Rule: aⁿ ÷ (a+1), where n is odd → remainder = **a**. Here a=5, divisor=5+1=6, n=99 (odd) → remainder = **5**.
- **Why A is wrong:** 4 has no derivation from this rule for the given values.
- **Why C is wrong:** 1 applies only when n is even (5⁹⁸ ÷ 6 → remainder 1). 99 is odd.
- **Why D is wrong:** 0 would mean 6 divides 5⁹⁹ exactly — impossible since gcd(5,6)=1.

### Q13 — Divisibility by 9 Implies What?
**Correct Answer: A) Divisible by 3**

- **Why A is correct:** 9 = 3². Every factor of 9 must also divide any multiple of 9. Since 3 divides 9, it divides every multiple of 9. Divisibility by 9 guarantees divisibility by 3. Always.
- **Why B is wrong:** Divisibility by 6 requires the number to be even. Numbers like 9, 27, 45 are divisible by 9 but are odd — not divisible by 6.
- **Why C is wrong:** 18 = 2 × 9. Same issue — odd multiples of 9 are not divisible by 18.
- **Why D is wrong:** 27 = 3³. A number can be divisible by 9 (like 18) without being divisible by 27.

### Q14 — Divisible by 4 but NOT by 8
**Correct Answer: C) 28**

- **Why C is correct:** 28 ÷ 4 = 7 ✅. Last 3 digits = 028; 028 ÷ 8 = 3.5 ✅ (not exact). So 28 is divisible by 4 but not by 8.
- **Why A is wrong:** 48 ÷ 8 = 6 — divisible by both 4 and 8.
- **Why B is wrong:** 32 ÷ 8 = 4 — divisible by both 4 and 8.
- **Why D is wrong:** 64 ÷ 8 = 8 — divisible by both 4 and 8.

> [!NOTE]
> A number divisible by 4 but not 8 must have exactly **two** factors of 2 in its prime factorization (2²). If it has three or more (2³=8), it becomes divisible by 8. 28 = 2² × 7 → exactly two factors of 2.

### Q15 — Last 2 Digits of 21⁴³
**Correct Answer: C) 61**

- **Why C is correct:** Base ends in 1 → unit digit is always 1. Tens digit = (tens digit of base × unit digit of exponent) mod 10 = (2 × 3) mod 10 = 6. Last 2 digits = **61**.
- **Why A is wrong:** 01 would require tens digit = 0, meaning 2 × (unit digit of 43) mod 10 = 0 → unit digit of 43 would need to be 0 or 5. Unit digit of 43 is 3.
- **Why B is wrong:** 41 would require tens digit = 4, meaning 2 × ? = 4 → unit digit of exponent = 2. But unit digit of 43 is 3, not 2.
- **Why D is wrong:** 81 would require (2 × ?) mod 10 = 8 → unit digit of exponent = 4. Unit digit of 43 is 3, not 4.

### Q16 — Divisibility Rule for 25
**Correct Answer: D) Its last 2 digits are divisible by 25**

- **Why D is correct:** 100 = 4 × 25 is divisible by 25. This means thousands, ten-thousands etc. always contribute 0 remainder when divided by 25. Only the last 2 digits determine divisibility by 25.
- **Why A is wrong:** Checking 1 last digit tests for divisibility by 5, not 25. (e.g., 35 ends in 5 but 35 ÷ 25 = 1.4 — not divisible.)
- **Why B is wrong:** Checking last 3 digits is the rule for 8 (since 1000 = 8 × 125).
- **Why C is wrong:** Digit sum rules belong to 3 and 9 only. 25 follows a positional rule, not a digit sum rule.

### Q17 — Remainder of 2¹⁰ ÷ 7
**Correct Answer: C) 2**

- **Why C is correct:** Fermat's Little Theorem: 2⁶ mod 7 = 1. So 2¹⁰ = 2⁶ × 2⁴ = 1 × 16. 16 mod 7: 16 − 14 = **2**.
- **Why A is wrong:** 4 = 2² in isolation. It is not the result of 16 mod 7.
- **Why B is wrong:** 3 has no derivation from 16 mod 7. 7×2=14, 16−14=2 not 3.
- **Why D is wrong:** 1 = 2⁶ mod 7 (pure Fermat result). But we have 2¹⁰, which has the extra 2⁴=16 to account for.

### Q18 — Divisibility of n(n+1)(n+2)
**Correct Answer: B) Always divisible by 6**

- **Why B is correct:** n(n+1)(n+2) is a product of 3 consecutive integers. Among any 3 consecutive integers, at least one is even (divisible by 2) and at least one is divisible by 3. Since gcd(2,3)=1, the product is always divisible by 2×3 = **6**.
- **Why A is wrong:** Not always by 4. Counter-example: 1×2×3=6. 6÷4=1.5 — not divisible by 4.
- **Why C is wrong:** Not always by 8. Same counter-example: 6÷8 is not an integer.
- **Why D is wrong:** Not always by 12. 6÷12 is not an integer — same counter-example holds.

- **Why B is wrong:** 30 minutes → LCM would need to be 30. But 30÷8=3.75 — 30 is not a multiple of 8.
- **Why C is wrong:** 48 minutes → 48÷10=4.8 — 48 is not a multiple of 10.
- **Why D is wrong:** 24 minutes → 24÷10=2.4 — 24 is not a multiple of 10.

### Q21 — BODMAS Simplification
**Correct Answer: B) 6**

  - Curly bracket: {20 ÷ 5 × 4} → left to right: 20÷5=4, then 4×4 = **16**
  - Square bracket: [40 − 16] = **24**
  - Final: 24 ÷ 4 = **6** ✅
- **Why A is wrong:** 2 would result from incorrectly applying addition before division inside the curly bracket.
- **Why C is wrong:** 4 results from stopping at 24÷6 or mishandling the bracket subtraction step.
- **Why D is wrong:** 10 results from skipping the innermost bracket evaluation and dividing incorrectly early.

- **Why B is wrong:** 13 would be the LCM only if 17 were a multiple of 13 — it is not.
- **Why C is wrong:** 17 would be the LCM only if 13 were a multiple of 17 — it is not.

### Q24 — HCF of 72, 108, and 180
**Correct Answer: B) 36**

- **Why B is correct:** 72=2³×3², 108=2²×3³, 180=2²×3²×5. HCF = lowest powers of common prime factors = 2²×3² = 4×9 = **36**.
- **Why A is wrong:** 18 = 2×3² — underestimates the power of 2 (should be 2², not 2¹).
- **Why C is wrong:** 54 = 2×3³ — uses 2¹ instead of 2² and 3³ instead of 3² (takes too high a power of 3).
- **Why D is wrong:** 72 = 2³×3². For HCF you need the **lowest** powers of common factors — 72 uses 2³ which is higher than 2² appearing in 108 and 180.

### Q25 — Trailing Zeros in 50!
**Correct Answer: B) 12**

- **Why B is correct:** Trailing zeros = count of (2,5) pairs = limited by 5s. ⌊50/5⌋ + ⌊50/25⌋ + ⌊50/125⌋ = 10 + 2 + 0 = **12**.
- **Why A is wrong:** 10 = ⌊50/5⌋ alone — forgets to add the extra 5 contributed by 25 and 50 (which are multiples of 25).
- **Why C is wrong:** 14 would require ⌊50/5⌋+⌊50/25⌋ = 10+2+2 — an arithmetic error (⌊50/25⌋ = 2, not 4).
- **Why D is wrong:** 8 is less than ⌊50/5⌋=10 — definitively too low with no mathematical basis.

### Q26 — Automorphic Number
**Correct Answer: B) 76**

- **Why A is wrong:** 36² = 1296. Last 2 digits = 96 ≠ 36. Not automorphic.
- **Why C is wrong:** 64² = 4096. Last 2 digits = 96 ≠ 64. Not automorphic.

### Q28 — Sum of Cubes of First 4 Natural Numbers
**Correct Answer: B) 100**

- **Why B is correct:** Formula: [n(n+1)/2]². For n=4: [4×5/2]² = [10]² = **100**. Direct check: 1³+2³+3³+4³ = 1+8+27+64 = **100** ✅.
- **Why A is wrong:** 64 = 4³ alone — only the last term, not the sum.
- **Why C is wrong:** 36 = [n(n+1)/2] without squaring → 4×5/2 = 10, not 36. Confusion with the sum of first n naturals formula.
- **Why D is wrong:** 144 = 12² — would mean n(n+1)/2 = 12, i.e., n≈4.4 — no integer match for n=4.

### Q29 — Highest Power of 2 in 16!
**Correct Answer: C) 15**

- **Why C is correct:** Highest power of prime p in n! = ⌊n/p⌋ + ⌊n/p²⌋ + ⌊n/p³⌋ + ...
  - ⌊16/2⌋ = 8
  - ⌊16/4⌋ = 4
  - ⌊16/8⌋ = 2
  - ⌊16/16⌋ = 1
  - ⌊16/32⌋ = 0
  - Total = 8+4+2+1 = **15** ✅
- **Why A is wrong:** 12 comes from stopping at ⌊16/2⌋+⌊16/4⌋+⌊16/8⌋ = 8+4+2=14... not even 12. Likely a miscalculation trap.
- **Why B is wrong:** 14 = 8+4+2 = stopping before adding ⌊16/16⌋=1. Incomplete application of the formula.
- **Why D is wrong:** 16 would mean one extra power — overcounting by 1. A classic off-by-one trap.

### Q30 — Perfect Number
**Correct Answer: D) 28**

- **Why D is correct:** A perfect number equals the sum of all its proper factors (excluding itself). Factors of 28 (excluding 28): 1, 2, 4, 7, 14. Sum = 1+2+4+7+14 = **28** ✅.
- **Why A is wrong:** 12 → proper factors: 1,2,3,4,6. Sum=16 ≠ 12. Abundant number (sum > itself).
- **Why B is wrong:** 16 → proper factors: 1,2,4,8. Sum=15 ≠ 16. Deficient number (sum < itself).
- **Why C is wrong:** 24 → proper factors: 1,2,3,4,6,8,12. Sum=36 ≠ 24. Abundant number.

> [!NOTE]
> The first four perfect numbers are: **6, 28, 496, 8128**. For MCQs, 6 and 28 are the most tested.
