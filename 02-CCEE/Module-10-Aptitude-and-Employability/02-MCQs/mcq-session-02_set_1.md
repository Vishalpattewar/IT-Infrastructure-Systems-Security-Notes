## 📖 Full Explanations

---

### Q01 — Nature of Ratio
**Correct Answer: C) No unit — it is a pure number**

- **Why C is correct:** A ratio compares two quantities of the same kind expressed in the same unit. Since the units cancel out during division, a ratio is a dimensionless, pure number.
- **Why A is wrong:** While the quantities must be in the same unit before forming a ratio, the ratio itself carries no unit.
- **Why B is wrong:** Both quantities must be in the same unit — different units cannot be directly compared in a ratio without conversion.
- **Why D is wrong:** No unit is retained in a ratio — not even the unit of the larger quantity.

---

### Q02 — Duplicate Ratio of 4:5
**Correct Answer: C) 16:25**

- **Why C is correct:** Duplicate ratio = square of each term. 4²:5² = 16:25.
- **Why A is wrong:** 8:10 = 2 × (4:5) — this is just scaling the ratio, not squaring it.
- **Why B is wrong:** 2:√5 = sub-duplicate ratio, i.e., square root of each term — opposite operation.
- **Why D is wrong:** 64:125 = 4³:5³ — this is the **triplicate** ratio, not duplicate.

---

### Q03 — Dividing ₹1,200 in Ratio 3:5
**Correct Answer: B) ₹450**

- **Why B is correct:** Total parts = 3+5 = 8. A's share = 1,200 × 3/8 = 1,200 × 0.375 = **₹450**.
- **Why A is wrong:** ₹300 = 1,200 × 3/12 — denominator taken as 12 instead of 8.
- **Why C is wrong:** ₹600 = 1,200/2 — equal sharing, ignoring the ratio entirely.
- **Why D is wrong:** ₹750 = 1,200 × 5/8 — this is B's share, not A's.

---

### Q04 — Combining A:B and B:C to get A:B:C
**Correct Answer: B) 15:20:24**

- **Why B is correct:** A:B = 3:4 and B:C = 5:6. Make B equal: multiply first ratio by 5 → A:B = 15:20. Multiply second ratio by 4 → B:C = 20:24. Combined: A:B:C = **15:20:24**.
- **Why A is wrong:** 3:4:6 uses B:C directly without making B common — the B terms (4 and 5) are different and cannot be combined directly.
- **Why C is wrong:** 3:5:6 incorrectly replaces B with the first term of B:C, losing the correct linkage.
- **Why D is wrong:** 12:20:24 multiplies the first ratio by 4 but doesn't align B correctly — B would be 12 in first and 20 in second, still not equal.

---

### Q05 — Compound Ratio of 2:3 and 4:5
**Correct Answer: B) 8:15**

- **Why B is correct:** Compound ratio = product of numerators : product of denominators = (2×4):(3×5) = **8:15**.
- **Why A is wrong:** 6:8 = adding the terms (2+4):(3+5) — ratios are multiplied, not added.
- **Why C is wrong:** 6:15 = (2+4):(3×5) — inconsistently adds numerators but multiplies denominators.
- **Why D is wrong:** 8:10 = (2×4):(3+5+2) — incorrect denominator calculation with no basis.

---

### Q06 — Sub-duplicate Ratio of 9:25
**Correct Answer: B) 3:5**

- **Why B is correct:** Sub-duplicate ratio = square root of each term. √9:√25 = **3:5**.
- **Why A is wrong:** 81:625 = 9²:25² — this is the **duplicate** ratio (squaring, not square-rooting).
- **Why C is wrong:** 27:125 = 9^(3/2):25^(3/2) — this is the sub-triplicate then triplicate — no standard ratio type.
- **Why D is wrong:** 4.5:12.5 = dividing by 2 — scaling down, not taking square roots.

---

### Q07 — Finding Missing Term: 3:x = 12:20
**Correct Answer: C) 5**

- **Why C is correct:** Cross multiply: 3 × 20 = x × 12 → 60 = 12x → x = **5**.
- **Why A is wrong:** x = 4 gives 3:4 ≠ 12:20 (12:20 = 3:5, not 3:4).
- **Why B is wrong:** x = 8 gives 3:8. Cross check: 3×20=60 but 8×12=96 ≠ 60.
- **Why D is wrong:** x = 6 gives 3:6 = 1:2. But 12:20 = 3:5 ≠ 1:2.

---

### Q08 — Fraction Ratio to Whole Number Ratio
**Correct Answer: D) 6:4:3**

- **Why D is correct:** LCM of denominators 2, 3, 4 = 12. Multiply each term by 12: (1/2)×12 : (1/3)×12 : (1/4)×12 = 6:4:3.
- **Why A is wrong:** 2:3:4 simply lists the denominators — no calculation applied.
- **Why B is wrong:** 4:3:2 = reversing the result — wrong order.
- **Why C is wrong:** 3:4:6 = LCM of numerators over each denominator applied incorrectly — swapping numerator logic.

---

### Q09 — Fundamental Rule of Proportion
**Correct Answer: B) a × d = b × c**

- **Why B is correct:** In a:b::c:d, the product of extremes (a×d) equals the product of means (b×c). This is the cross-multiplication rule — the foundation of all proportion problems.
- **Why A is wrong:** a+d = b+c has no general validity in proportion. Example: 1:2::3:6 → 1+6=7 but 2+3=5 → not equal.
- **Why C is wrong:** a−d = b−c similarly has no general validity.
- **Why D is wrong:** a/d = b/c is invertendo — a valid property but only after the fundamental rule; it's not the primary rule.

---

### Q10 — Mean Proportional Between 9 and 25
**Correct Answer: B) 15**

- **Why B is correct:** Mean proportional = √(a×b) = √(9×25) = √225 = **15**.
- **Why A is wrong:** 17 = (9+25)/2 — this is the arithmetic mean, not the mean proportional (geometric mean).
- **Why C is wrong:** 225 = 9×25 — the product before taking the square root; forgot to apply √.
- **Why D is wrong:** 34 = 9+25 — plain addition with no mathematical basis for mean proportional.

---

### Q11 — Third Proportional to 4 and 8
**Correct Answer: C) 16**

- **Why C is correct:** Third proportional c = b²/a = 8²/4 = 64/4 = **16**. Verify: 4:8 = 8:16 ✅ (all equal 1:2).
- **Why A is wrong:** 12 = 8+4 — plain addition, no proportional logic.
- **Why B is wrong:** 2 = 8/4 — this is the ratio value, not the third proportional.
- **Why D is wrong:** 32 = 8×4 — product of given numbers, not the third proportional formula result.

---

### Q12 — Fourth Proportional to 3, 6, and 9
**Correct Answer: B) 18**

- **Why B is correct:** Fourth proportional d = (b×c)/a = (6×9)/3 = 54/3 = **18**. Verify: 3:6 = 9:18 ✅ (both = 1:2).
- **Why A is wrong:** 12 = (3+9)/2×4/3 — no standard formula gives this; likely guesswork.
- **Why C is wrong:** 27 = 9×3 — multiplying last two terms instead of applying the formula.
- **Why D is wrong:** 15 = 3+6+9−3 — arithmetic manipulation with no basis in proportion formula.

---

### Q13 — Componendo & Dividendo: a/b = 5/3
**Correct Answer: C) 4**

- **Why C is correct:** Componendo & Dividendo: (a+b)/(a−b) = (5+3)/(5−3) = 8/2 = **4**.
- **Why A is wrong:** 2 = (5−3)/(5−3) — subtracting both numerator and denominator makes no sense here.
- **Why B is wrong:** 3 = the denominator of the original ratio, not the result of the formula.
- **Why D is wrong:** 5 = the numerator of the original ratio alone — the formula was not applied.

---

### Q14 — Extremes in a Proportion
**Correct Answer: B) Extremes**

- **Why B is correct:** In a:b::c:d, the first and last terms (a and d) are called **extremes** (they are at the outer edges of the proportion).
- **Why A is wrong:** Means are the middle two terms — b and c — not a and d.
- **Why C is wrong:** Antecedents are the first terms of each ratio (a and c), not specifically a and d together.
- **Why D is wrong:** Consequents are the second terms of each ratio (b and d), not a and d together.

---

### Q15 — Continued Proportion Property
**Correct Answer: B) b² = ac**

- **Why B is correct:** If a:b = b:c (continued proportion), then by cross multiplication: b×b = a×c → **b² = ac**. b is the geometric mean of a and c.
- **Why A is wrong:** a² = bc — this confuses which term is squared. It's the middle term b that is squared, not a.
- **Why C is wrong:** c² = ab — again, wrong term squared; the middle term b is always the one that satisfies the square relationship.
- **Why D is wrong:** ac = a+c — addition has no basis in proportion; the relationship is multiplicative (b² = ac), not additive.

---

### Q16 — Componendo & Dividendo: (x+y)/(x−y) = 7/3
**Correct Answer: D) 5:2**

- **Why D is correct:** Given (x+y)/(x−y) = 7/3. By Componendo & Dividendo applied in reverse: x/y = (7+3)/(7−3) = 10/4 = **5:2**.
- **Why A is wrong:** 4:1 = (7+1)/(7−1)/2 — incorrect reverse application of the formula.
- **Why B is wrong:** 7:3 is the given ratio of (x+y) to (x−y) — not the ratio of x to y.
- **Why C is wrong:** 3:2 confuses the denominator value (3) with the result and divides arbitrarily.

---

### Q17 — Direct Variation: Find x when y = 7
**Correct Answer: C) 35**

- **Why C is correct:** Direct variation → x = ky. When x=10, y=2: k = 10/2 = 5. When y=7: x = 5×7 = **35**.
- **Why A is wrong:** 14 = 2×7 — uses y directly without applying the constant k=5.
- **Why B is wrong:** 70 = 10×7 — multiplies original x by new y with no mathematical basis.
- **Why D is wrong:** 3.5 = 7/2 — divides new y by original y; inverts the relationship incorrectly.

---

### Q18 — Inverse Variation: Find y when x = 8
**Correct Answer: C) 3**

- **Why C is correct:** Inverse variation → xy = k. k = 6×4 = 24. When x=8: y = 24/8 = **3**.
- **Why A is wrong:** 2 = 8/4 — divides new x by original y; misapplies the formula.
- **Why B is wrong:** 4 = original y; fails to use the new x value (8) at all.
- **Why D is wrong:** 6 = original x; copies original x as the answer without any calculation.

---

### Q19 — Inverse Proportion: Workers and Days
**Correct Answer: B) 15**

- **Why B is correct:** More workers → fewer days (inverse proportion). 6×10 = 4×d → d = 60/4 = **15 days**.
- **Why A is wrong:** 12 = (6/4)×(10−2) — mixing subtraction with ratio; no valid formula.
- **Why C is wrong:** 20 = (6÷3)×10 — incorrect scaling of the number of workers.
- **Why D is wrong:** 8 = 10 − (6−4) — subtracting workers from days; dimensionally meaningless.

---

### Q20 — Joint Variation: Find z when x=5, y=4
**Correct Answer: C) 40**

- **Why C is correct:** Joint variation → z = kxy. When z=12, x=3, y=2: k = 12/(3×2) = 2. When x=5, y=4: z = 2×5×4 = **40**.
- **Why A is wrong:** 20 = k×(5+4+1) — adds instead of multiplies x and y.
- **Why B is wrong:** 30 = 2×(5+4+6) — incorrect arithmetic.
- **Why D is wrong:** 60 = 2×5×6 — uses y=6 instead of y=4.

---

### Q21 — Direct Variation Graph
**Correct Answer: C) A straight line through origin**

- **Why C is correct:** In direct variation, x = ky. This is a linear equation with zero intercept (c=0 in y=mx+c). The graph is a **straight line passing through the origin**.
- **Why A is wrong:** A hyperbola is the graph of **inverse** variation (xy = k).
- **Why B is wrong:** A parabola represents quadratic relationships (y = x²), not direct variation.
- **Why D is wrong:** A straight line NOT through the origin represents a partial variation (y = kx + c where c ≠ 0), not pure direct variation.

---

### Q22 — Transitivity of Variation
**Correct Answer: B) x ∝ z**

- **Why B is correct:** If x ∝ y, then x = k₁y. If y ∝ z, then y = k₂z. Substituting: x = k₁k₂z → x ∝ z. Variation is transitive.
- **Why A is wrong:** x ∝ 1/z would mean x and z are inversely related — that only holds if x ∝ y and y ∝ 1/z (or vice versa), not both direct.
- **Why C is wrong:** x+y ∝ z has no standard rule in variation theory from these premises.
- **Why D is wrong:** x/z = constant is actually true (it follows from x ∝ z), but the condition "only if x=y" is completely wrong — no such restriction exists.

---

### Q23 — Simple Partnership: Find A's Share
**Correct Answer: C) ₹6,000**

- **Why C is correct:** Same time period → profit ratio = investment ratio = 30,000:50,000 = 3:5. A's share = 16,000 × 3/8 = **₹6,000**.
- **Why A is wrong:** ₹4,000 = 16,000 × 1/4 — uses ratio 1:3 with no basis.
- **Why B is wrong:** ₹8,000 = 16,000/2 — equal sharing, ignoring the ratio.
- **Why D is wrong:** ₹10,000 = 16,000 × 5/8 — this is B's share, not A's.

---

### Q24 — Compound Partnership: Profit-Sharing Ratio
**Correct Answer: C) 1:1**

- **Why C is correct:** A's equivalent = 20,000×6 = 1,20,000. B's equivalent = 30,000×4 = 1,20,000. Ratio = 1,20,000:1,20,000 = **1:1**.
- **Why A is wrong:** 2:3 = ratio of investments alone (20,000:30,000) — ignores time, which is the key variable in compound partnership.
- **Why B is wrong:** 3:2 = inverse of investment ratio — no basis when time is factored in.
- **Why D is wrong:** 2:1 = 6:4 (time ratio alone) — ignores investment amounts.

---

### Q25 — Working Partner Salary — Correct Procedure
**Correct Answer: C) Deduct total salary from total profit first, then divide the remainder**

- **Why C is correct:** The working partner earns a salary as compensation for managing the business. This is deducted from total profit before dividing, because salary is not part of the profit-sharing arrangement.
- **Why A is wrong:** Adding salary to profit inflates the total — no logical basis; it increases what's being divided, not what was earned.
- **Why B is wrong:** Dividing first then deducting gives the same final number but is procedurally wrong and leads to incorrect calculations in multi-partner scenarios.
- **Why D is wrong:** Ignoring salary entirely means the working partner is underpaid — it violates the partnership agreement.

---

### Q26 — Simple Partnership: Find B's Share
**Correct Answer: C) ₹15,000**

- **Why C is correct:** Ratio = 40,000:60,000 = 2:3. B's share = 25,000 × 3/5 = **₹15,000**.
- **Why A is wrong:** ₹10,000 = 25,000 × 2/5 — this is A's share (ratio 2 part), not B's (ratio 3 part).
- **Why B is wrong:** ₹12,000 = 25,000 × 12/25 — incorrect fraction with no basis.
- **Why D is wrong:** ₹18,000 = 25,000 × 3.6/5 — overestimates B's share beyond the correct proportion.

---

### Q27 — New Partner Joins After 4 Months
**Correct Answer: B) 25:24**

- **Why B is correct:** A invests for all 12 months. B joins after 4 months → B invests for 8 months.
  - A's equivalent = 50,000 × 12 = 6,00,000
  - B's equivalent = 60,000 × 8 = 4,80,000
  - Ratio = 6,00,000 : 4,80,000 = 600:480 = **25:24** ✅
- **Why A is wrong:** 5:6 = ratio of investments alone (50,000:60,000) — ignores different time periods entirely.
- **Why C is wrong:** 5:4 = 12:8 (time ratio only) — ignores investment amounts.
- **Why D is wrong:** 25:18 = 50,000×12 : 60,000×9 — uses 9 months for B instead of the correct 8.

---

### Q28 — Profit Ratio: A=₹24,000 for 8 months, B=₹36,000 for 6 months
**Correct Answer: A) 8:9**

- **Why A is correct:**
  - A's equivalent = 24,000 × 8 = 1,92,000
  - B's equivalent = 36,000 × 6 = 2,16,000
  - Ratio = 1,92,000 : 2,16,000 = 192:216 = **8:9** ✅ (divide both by 24,000)
- **Why B is wrong:** 2:3 = investment ratio alone (24,000:36,000) — time not factored in.
- **Why C is wrong:** 3:2 = inverse of investment ratio — no basis.
- **Why D is wrong:** 4:3 = 8:6 (time ratio) — ignores investment amounts.

---

### Q29 — Working Partner Salary + Profit Share
**Correct Answer: B) ₹9,600**

- **Why B is correct:**
  - Working partner (A) annual salary = 3,000 × 12 = ₹36,000
  - Remaining profit = 60,000 − 36,000 = ₹24,000
  - Profit ratio = 3:2 (total parts = 5)
  - B's share = 24,000 × 2/5 = **₹9,600** ✅
- **Why A is wrong:** ₹24,000 = remaining profit before distribution — B gets only 2/5 of it, not all of it.
- **Why C is wrong:** ₹12,000 = 60,000 × 2/5 (without deducting salary first) — the most common trap answer; salary was not deducted before distributing.
- **Why D is wrong:** ₹14,400 = 24,000 × 3/5 — this is A's share of the remaining profit (not B's).

---

### Q30 — How Loss is Shared in a Partnership
**Correct Answer: C) The same ratio as profit unless stated otherwise**

- **Why C is correct:** By default in a partnership, both profits AND losses are shared in the same agreed-upon ratio (typically proportional to investment × time). The law treats gains and losses symmetrically unless the agreement specifies otherwise.
- **Why A is wrong:** Equal sharing regardless of investment violates the fundamental principle of proportional partnership — it only applies if investments are equal or explicitly agreed.
- **Why B is wrong:** Inverse of investment ratio has no basis — a partner who invests more bears more risk, not less.
- **Why D is wrong:** Time alone doesn't determine the loss-sharing ratio — both capital AND time (for compound partnerships) are considered.
